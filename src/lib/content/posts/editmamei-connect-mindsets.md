Editmamei Connect now changes what happens when you ask for something open. Say "make it better"
or "fix this up" and your assistant no longer reaches for a safe, generic correction. It looks at
the photo, takes on a mindset that fits it, and edits the way that kind of photographer would:
where the crop goes, what the light should do, how far the color can go. Before it starts, it
tells you which mindset it chose and what it plans to change, and waits for your go.

## The mindsets

**The Family Photographer** is the default for everyday photos: trips, days out, streets, pets and
the people in them. Bright, clear and vivid, like a good camera took your phone photo, and it never
removes or reshapes a person. **The Darkroom Printer** is for strong light and deep shadow, texture,
and photos that would hold up printed large or in black and white. **The Cinematographer** is for a
scene that already has a film's drama: hard low light, night, fog, a lone figure. **The
Documentarian** keeps a record true to how it happened, for street moments, events and family
history.

Two more are about where a photo is going, so they start only when you name them or say what the
photo is for: **the Art Director** for covers, banners, products and ads, and **the Feed Creator**
for anything headed to a social feed.

## Getting the most from them

- **Say what the photo is for.** "For our Airbnb listing", "for the wedding album" or "for
  Instagram" changes the rules the edit follows, and they come before any style.
- **Name one when you know what you want.** "Edit this like a darkroom printer" or "give it the
  Cinematographer's noir" goes straight there. Each mindset has its own styles, from a warm summer
  day to platinum-toned black and white.
- **Tell it what matters in the frame.** "The dog is the subject" or "keep the church in" lets it
  protect what counts: a crop that would cut the subject off is refused before it runs, and a step
  that leaves the subject darker than its surroundings or clips the highlights gets corrected.
- **Compare two takes.** Ask for the Documentarian's version, then "now try it as the
  Cinematographer", and you get both to choose from.
- **Edit a whole set as one.** "Edit these twelve as a set" gives the first photo the full
  treatment, then matches the rest to it, so the album looks like one shoot.

Every mindset can also use clarity now: local contrast that makes texture and detail stand out,
without the clipping and darkening that heavy sharpening leaves behind.

If you'd rather your assistant just do exactly what you ask, turn mindsets off on the Settings page
under "How your assistant edits". You can still ask for one by name at any time.
